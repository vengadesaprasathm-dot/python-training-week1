# Five-Minute Presentation – Speaking Plan

Use the actual findings from `report/Findings_Summary.md`. Add three images from:
`../Day5_Task3_Five_Visualisations/reports/figures/`

## Slide 1 – Business question and objective (45 seconds)
- What employee/workforce patterns are we trying to understand?
- Objective: compare department income and explore employee characteristics.
- Explain that this is a practice dataset and the analysis is descriptive.

## Slide 2 – Dataset and method (45 seconds)
- Mention the number of rows and columns from the generated findings summary.
- Explain that Pandas was used for cleaning, grouping, summary statistics and correlations.
- Mention that Matplotlib/Seaborn were used to create five visualisations.

## Slide 3 – Three charts and insights (2 minutes)
Insert three charts:
1. `01_bar_average_income_by_department.png` – compare average monthly income by department.
2. `03_scatter_age_vs_income.png` – discuss whether age and income show a visible pattern.
3. `05_boxplot_income_by_department.png` – compare medians, spread and possible outliers.

Use the exact numbers from your generated summary/notebook. Do not guess values.

## Slide 4 – Recommendations (1 minute)
- Present the two recommendations from `Findings_Summary.md`.
- Explain how each recommendation follows from the evidence.
- Emphasise that management should validate findings before taking action.

## Slide 5 – Conclusion (30 seconds)
- Recap the business question and the most important finding.
- Mention the main limitation: small practice dataset.
- Thank the trainer and invite questions.

## Short opening script
“Good morning. My project analyses an employee dataset to understand workforce patterns and compare monthly income across departments. I used Python, Pandas, Matplotlib and Seaborn. I will show three key visual findings and two recommendations based on the available data.”

## Short closing script
“To conclude, the analysis highlights differences in department income and employee patterns. The recommendations are starting points for further investigation, not final HR decisions, because the dataset is limited. Thank you.”
